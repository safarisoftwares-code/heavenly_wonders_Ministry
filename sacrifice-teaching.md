# The Two Sides of Sacrifice: Physical and Spiritual

## A Biblical Survey

### I. The Principle Stated

Sacrifice in Scripture is never merely an external act. From the beginning, God has weighed the heart behind the gift. The physical offering is the visible expression; the spiritual surrender is the accepted reality.

"To obey is better than sacrifice, and to heed is better than the fat of rams." — 1 Samuel 15:22

"The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit; a broken and contrite heart, O God, you will not despise." — Psalm 51:17

### II. The Old Testament Pattern

1. Cain and Abel — the first sacrifice, the first dividing line (Genesis 4:1-7)
2. Noah — sacrifice that pleased God (Genesis 8:20-21)
3. Abraham and Isaac — the physical withheld, the spiritual accepted (Genesis 22:1-18; Hebrews 11:17-19)
4. The Passover — blood on the doorposts (Exodus 12:1-13)
5. The Levitical offerings — five kinds, one principle (Leviticus 1-7)
6. David — the heart above the altar (Psalm 51)
7. Micah — the summary of the matter (Micah 6:6-8)
8. Isaiah — sacrifice without the heart is offensive (Isaiah 1:11-17)
9. The widow's mite — the measure is the heart, not the amount (Mark 12:41-44)
10. Ananias and Sapphira — the physical act without the spiritual truth (Acts 5:1-11)
11. The Philippian church — giving that pleases God (Philippians 4:10-20)

### III. The New Testament Fulfilment

1. Christ — the once-for-all sacrifice (Hebrews 10:1-14)
2. The believer's living sacrifice (Romans 12:1-2)
3. Spiritual sacrifices (1 Peter 2:4-5, 9)
4. The sacrifice of praise and thanksgiving (Hebrews 13:15-16; Psalm 116:17)
5. The sacrifice of a broken heart (Psalm 51:17; 2 Corinthians 7:10)

### IV. The Doctrine Summarised

1. Sacrifice is not primarily about what is given; it is about what is surrendered.
2. The physical is the visible sign; the spiritual is the accepted reality.
3. God can accept the spiritual without consuming the physical — as with Isaac.
4. The eternal accounting is not the earthly accounting.
5. The physical without the spiritual is religious performance.
6. The spiritual without the physical is empty sentiment.
7. True sacrifice is both at once.
8. Blessing follows the accepted sacrifice.

### V. Practical Illustrations for Today

- The altar and the money
- The food and the produce
- The musician who plays
- The one who serves unseen
- The one who gives and sees no return
- The one who gives reluctantly

### VI. A Closing Word

The altar is a place of two realities. What is laid on it is seen by men. What is surrendered in the heart is seen by God.

## Footer Topic: The Sacrifice of Praise — Worship as an Offering

"Through Jesus, therefore, let us continually offer to God a sacrifice of praise — the fruit of lips that openly profess his name." — Hebrews 13:15

Praise is called a sacrifice because it costs something. The physical is the voice, the song, the lifted hands, the spoken word. The spiritual is the trust, the love, the faith that praises God not for what He gives but for who He is.
